# Isomorphism Between First and Second Quantization

First quantization gives the wavefunction spaces for each total particle number $N$:

$$
\mathcal H_x^N
=\operatorname{span}\left\{\bigotimes_{i=1}^N\varphi_{k_i}(x_i):
\varphi_{k_i}\in\mathcal P_x(\mathcal H_x)\right\},
$$

and the physical space:

$$
\widetilde{\mathcal H}_x^N
=\operatorname{span}\left\{\Psi_{k_1,\ldots,k_N}(X)
=\dfrac1{\sqrt{N!\prod_k n_k!}}
\sum_P(\pm)^P\prod_{i=1}^N\varphi_{k_i}(x_{Pi})\right\}.
$$

Here $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_N)$, $dX=\prod_i dx_i$, and $\operatorname{span}$ is understood as the closed span in the Hilbert space. The single-particle basis $\mathcal P_x(\mathcal H_x)$ is defined in [[0. 一次量子化#^def-single-particle-coordinate-basis|单粒子基]]. The physical space and its wavefunctions are those in [[0. 一次量子化#③ 全同子空间]]: $n_k$ counts the occurrences of $k$, $P$ runs over all permutations, and $(\pm)^P$ is the Bose or Fermi sign.

Fix $N\ge1$ and choose the same statistics on both sides. Write $\widetilde{\mathcal H}^N$ for the abstract [[2. Symmetrizer and Antisymmetrizer#^def-bose-fermi-space|Bose or Fermi space]], and $\widetilde{\mathcal H}_x^N$ for its wavefunction counterpart. Each independent basis state is counted once: the labels are nondecreasing for Bose and strictly increasing for Fermi.

We just need a [[幺正算符与厄米算符的区别#逐项对照|unitary]] transition from $|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle$ to $\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N}$ to prove the isomorphism. Here $\lambda_i$ plays the same role as $k_i$ above. Let's give the detailed mathematical form of it step by step.

**GOAL:** Prove $\widetilde{\mathcal H}^N\cong\widetilde{\mathcal H}_x^N$ by constructing a unitary map.

## 1. Definition of $U_N$

Actually, we can directly make a definition:

> [!def] Map $U_N$
> On the independent normalized basis states, define
> $$
> U_N:\widetilde{\mathcal H}^N\longrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal H}_x^N,
> \qquad U_N|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle
> :=\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N},
> $$
> and extend by linearity to basis expansions:
> $$
> U_N\left(\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle\right)
> :=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\Psi_\alpha.
> $$
> Here $\alpha$ denotes a complete ordered list $(\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)$, $|\alpha\rangle:=|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle$, and $\Psi_\alpha:=\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N}$; $c_\alpha$ are complex expansion coefficients.
^def-unitary-coordinate-map

The image is a function; its value at $X$ is

$$
\bigl(U_N|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle\bigr)(X)
=\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N}(X).
$$

Why does this make sense? Each independent basis vector is assigned one wavefunction, and the expansion in an independent basis is unique. Thus the linear extension specifies one image for each state.

The two complete bases correspond one to one. Given any wavefunction $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\Psi_\alpha$, its preimage is $\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle$; conversely, equal images have equal expansion coefficients and hence equal preimages. So this map is a bijection: it is both one-to-one and onto.

But we cannot stop here. To obtain a unitary isomorphism, we still need to check that this transition keeps inner products, so that we can later use it for the second quantization of operators.

## 2. Check Inner Product

Take any two abstract states $|\Phi_N\rangle,|\Psi_N\rangle\in\widetilde{\mathcal H}^N$. Expand them in the independent normalized basis:

$$
|\Phi_N\rangle=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle,
\qquad
|\Psi_N\rangle=\sum_\beta d_\beta|\beta\rangle.
$$

Here $\beta$ labels the same basis as $\alpha$, and $c_\alpha=\langle\alpha|\Phi_N\rangle$, $d_\beta=\langle\beta|\Psi_N\rangle$ are their respective coefficients. By the definition of $U_N$, write the corresponding wavefunctions as

$$
\begin{aligned}
\Phi_N(X)&:=\bigl(U_N|\Phi_N\rangle\bigr)(X)
=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\Psi_\alpha(X),\\
\Psi_N(X)&:=\bigl(U_N|\Psi_N\rangle\bigr)(X)
=\sum_\beta d_\beta\Psi_\beta(X).
\end{aligned}
$$

The ket $|\Psi_N\rangle$ is the abstract state; $\Psi_N$ is its image as a function.

> [!def] Inner product in the wavefunction space
> $$
> \langle\Phi_N,\Psi_N\rangle_x
> :=\int dX\,\Phi_N^*(X)\Psi_N(X).
> $$
^def-coordinate-wavefunction-inner-product

With two independent coordinate variables, the same inner product reads

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\Phi_N,\Psi_N\rangle_x
&=\int dX\,dX'\,\Phi_N^*(X)\delta(X-X')\Psi_N(X')\\
&=\int dX\,\Phi_N^*(X)\Psi_N(X).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $\delta(X-X')=\prod_i\delta(x_i-x_i')$; integrating over $X'$ gives $\int dX'\,\delta(X-X')\Psi_N(X')=\Psi_N(X)$. This is why the single integral uses the same $X$ for both functions.

Now use the [[0. 一次量子化#^prop-identical-coordinate-orthonormality|orthonormality of the wavefunction basis]]:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\Phi_N,\Psi_N\rangle_x
&=\int dX\,
\left(\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^*\Psi_\alpha^*(X)\right)
\left(\sum_\beta d_\beta\Psi_\beta(X)\right)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_\alpha^*d_\beta
\underbrace{\int dX\,\Psi_\alpha^*(X)\Psi_\beta(X)}_{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}\\
&=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^*d_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

On the other hand, the abstract basis is orthonormal by [[3. Basis of Bose and Fermi Spaces#^prop-bose-basis|the Bose basis construction]] or [[3. Basis of Bose and Fermi Spaces#^prop-fermi-basis|the Fermi basis construction]]. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
\langle\Phi_N|\Psi_N\rangle
&=\left(\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^*\langle\alpha|\right)
\left(\sum_\beta d_\beta|\beta\rangle\right)\\
&=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}c_\alpha^*d_\beta
\underbrace{\langle\alpha|\beta\rangle}_{\delta_{\alpha\beta}}\\
&=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha^*d_\alpha.
\end{aligned}
$$

We have computed the inner products in the two spaces separately and obtained the same number. Thus

$$
\left\langle U_N|\Phi_N\rangle,\,U_N|\Psi_N\rangle\right\rangle_x
=\langle\Phi_N|\Psi_N\rangle.
$$

> [!done] Unitarity of $U_N$
> Because $U_N$ preserves inner products and is onto, it is unitary.
^prop-unitary-coordinate-map

## 3. Writing the Map with Field Operators

We have defined $U_N$ by matching basis states. Can we obtain the corresponding wavefunction directly from the state, using the field operators introduced in [[8. Field Operator#Definition]]?

### 1. Start with One Particle

The field operator is $\hat\psi(x)=\sum_\mu\varphi_\mu(x)a_\mu$. By [[5. Creation and Annihilation#2. If $N=1$|single-particle annihilation]],

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat\psi(x)|\lambda\rangle
&=\sum_\mu\varphi_\mu(x)a_\mu|\lambda\rangle\\
&=\sum_\mu\varphi_\mu(x)\delta_{\mu\lambda}|0\rangle
=\varphi_\lambda(x)|0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $\langle0|0\rangle=1$, we obtain

$$
\langle0|\hat\psi(x)|\lambda\rangle=\varphi_\lambda(x).
$$

The field operator removes the particle, and the coefficient of the vacuum is exactly the single-particle wavefunction. For $N$ particles, it is natural to apply $N$ annihilation fields and then take the inner product with the vacuum.

> [!def] Vacuum amplitude $C_N$
> For a normalized $N$-particle basis state, define
> $$
> C_N(x_1,\ldots,x_N;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)
> :=\langle0|\hat\psi(x_N)\cdots\hat\psi(x_1)
> |\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle.
> $$
> This is the coefficient of the vacuum after applying the $N$ annihilation fields, with $\hat\psi(x_1)$ acting first.
^def-normalized-basis-vacuum-amplitude

This gives a function of $N$ coordinates, but we still need to compare it with the normalized wavefunction. Start with two particles.

### 2. Two Particles: Finding the Factor

Use [[6. Commutation Relations#^prop-mixed-relations|the creation and annihilation relations]], with $\eta=+1$ for Bose and $\eta=-1$ for Fermi:

$$
a_\mu a_\lambda^\dagger
=\delta_{\mu\lambda}I+\eta a_\lambda^\dagger a_\mu.
$$

Substituting the field expansion gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat\psi(x)a_\lambda^\dagger
&=\sum_\mu\varphi_\mu(x)
\left(\delta_{\mu\lambda}I+\eta a_\lambda^\dagger a_\mu\right)\\
&=\varphi_\lambda(x)I+\eta a_\lambda^\dagger\hat\psi(x).
\end{aligned}
$$

First calculate on the product of creation operators, before inserting its normalization factor:

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\hat\psi(x_1)a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger a_{\lambda_2}^\dagger|0\rangle\\
&=\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)a_{\lambda_2}^\dagger|0\rangle
+\eta a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\hat\psi(x_1)a_{\lambda_2}^\dagger|0\rangle\\
&=\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)a_{\lambda_2}^\dagger|0\rangle
+\eta\varphi_{\lambda_2}(x_1)a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger|0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The remaining term vanishes because $\hat\psi(x_1)|0\rangle=0$. Applying $\langle0|\hat\psi(x_2)$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\langle0|\hat\psi(x_2)\hat\psi(x_1)
a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger a_{\lambda_2}^\dagger|0\rangle\\
&=\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)\varphi_{\lambda_2}(x_2)
+\eta\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_2)\varphi_{\lambda_2}(x_1).
\end{aligned}
$$

The two terms of the symmetric or antisymmetric wavefunction appear automatically. Restoring the [[5. Creation and Annihilation#^prop-creation-from-vacuum|basis normalization]] and comparing with [[0. 一次量子化#③ 全同子空间|the coordinate wavefunction]],

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_2(x_1,x_2;\lambda_1,\lambda_2)
&=\dfrac{\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)\varphi_{\lambda_2}(x_2)
+\eta\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_2)\varphi_{\lambda_2}(x_1)}
{\sqrt{\prod_\lambda n_\lambda!}}\\
&=\sqrt{2!}\,\Psi_{\lambda_1,\lambda_2}(x_1,x_2).
\end{aligned}
$$

For $N$ particles, this suggests the factor $\sqrt{N!}$. We will prove it by induction.

### 3. Generalizing to an Arbitrary $N$-Particle Space

To obtain a recursion, first move $\hat\psi(x_1)$ through the product of creation operators. Repeatedly using the field relation above gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\hat\psi(x_1)a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger|0\rangle\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^N\eta^{j-1}\varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\cdots\widehat{a_{\lambda_j}^\dagger}\cdots
a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger|0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

The hat means deleting that factor while keeping the others in their original order. To reach the $j$-th creation operator, the annihilation field passes $j-1$ preceding factors, giving $\eta^{j-1}$. The term in which it passes all factors vanishes on the vacuum.

However, $C_N$ is defined using normalized basis states. Before writing a recursion for $C_N$, we must account for the normalization after removing one label.

By [[5. Creation and Annihilation#^prop-creation-from-vacuum|constructing basis states from the vacuum]],

$$
|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle
=\dfrac1{\sqrt D}
a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger|0\rangle,
\qquad D:=\prod_\lambda n_\lambda!.
$$

Suppose we remove the entry $\lambda_j$, whose occupation number before removal is $n:=n_{\lambda_j}\ge1$. The occupation number changes from $n$ to $n-1$, but the normalization contains factorials. After removal, the factorial product is

$$
D_j:=(n-1)!\prod_{\lambda\ne\lambda_j}n_\lambda!
=\dfrac Dn.
$$

Thus the ratio is $(n-1)!/n!=1/n$, not $(n-1)/n$. In particular, the remaining product of creation operators is

$$
a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\cdots
\widehat{a_{\lambda_j}^\dagger}\cdots
a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger|0\rangle
=\sqrt{D_j}\,
|\lambda_1\cdots\widehat{\lambda_j}\cdots\lambda_N\rangle.
$$

The hat over an entry means that it is deleted; all remaining entries keep their original order. Multiplying by the original prefactor $1/\sqrt D$ gives

$$
\dfrac1{\sqrt D}
a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger\cdots
\widehat{a_{\lambda_j}^\dagger}\cdots
a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger|0\rangle
=\sqrt{\dfrac{D_j}{D}}\,
|\lambda_1\cdots\widehat{\lambda_j}\cdots\lambda_N\rangle
=\dfrac1{\sqrt{n_{\lambda_j}}}
|\lambda_1\cdots\widehat{\lambda_j}\cdots\lambda_N\rangle.
$$

This is the normalization factor for one term in a sum over positions $j$. For Bose, a label with occupation number $n$ occurs at $n$ positions. All $n$ removal terms give the same remaining state, so their combined factor is

$$
n\cdot\dfrac1{\sqrt n}=\sqrt n,
$$

in agreement with [[5. Creation and Annihilation#^prop-bose-annihilation|annihilation on the Bose basis]]. For example, when $n=3$, each position contributes $\sqrt{2!/3!}=1/\sqrt3$, and the three contributions sum to $\sqrt3$. For a nonzero Fermi basis state, $n_{\lambda_j}=1$; the exchange sign is still required, as in [[5. Creation and Annihilation#^prop-fermi-annihilation|annihilation on the Fermi basis]].

Substituting this normalization factor into the field action gives

$$
\hat\psi(x_1)|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle
=\sum_{j=1}^N
\dfrac{\eta^{j-1}}{\sqrt{n_{\lambda_j}}}
\varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
|\lambda_1\cdots\widehat{\lambda_j}\cdots\lambda_N\rangle,
$$

where $\eta=+1$ for Bose and $\eta=-1$ for Fermi. The sign $\eta^{j-1}$ comes from moving the annihilation operator past the first $j-1$ creation operators, using [[6. Commutation Relations#^prop-mixed-relations|the creation and annihilation relations]].

Applying $\langle0|\hat\psi(x_N)\cdots\hat\psi(x_2)$ to the preceding equation gives

> [!done] Recursion for $C_N$
> For $N\ge2$,
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> &C_N(x_1,\ldots,x_N;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)\\
> &=\sum_{j=1}^N\dfrac{\eta^{j-1}}{\sqrt{n_{\lambda_j}}}
> \varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
> C_{N-1}(x_2,\ldots,x_N;
> \lambda_1,\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_j},\ldots,\lambda_N).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Each $n_{\lambda_j}$ is the occupation number before removal. The starting case is $C_1(x_1;\lambda_1)=\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)$.
^prop-normalized-vacuum-amplitude-recursion

The starting case follows from $a_\lambda|\lambda_1\rangle=\delta_{\lambda,\lambda_1}|0\rangle$ in [[5. Creation and Annihilation#2. If $N=1$|the single-particle annihilation formula]]. The sum in the recursion is over positions, so each term contains $1/\sqrt{n_{\lambda_j}}$; the Bose factor $\sqrt{n_\lambda}$ appears only after combining the terms with the same label.

### 4. Induction: From $N$ to $N+1$

The starting case $C_1(x_1;\lambda_1)=\varphi_{\lambda_1}(x_1)$ has already been checked. Suppose that for every allowed list of $N$ labels,

$$
C_N(y_1,\ldots,y_N;\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)
=\sqrt{N!}\,\Psi_{\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N}(y_1,\ldots,y_N).
$$

We use $\mu$ and $y$ because the induction hypothesis applies to any list of remaining labels and coordinates. For $N+1$ particles, the recursion reads

$$
\begin{aligned}
&C_{N+1}(X;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{N+1})\\
&=\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\dfrac{\eta^{j-1}}{\sqrt{n_{\lambda_j}}}
\varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
C_N(x_2,\ldots,x_{N+1};
\lambda_1,\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_j},\ldots,\lambda_{N+1}),
\end{aligned}
$$

where now $X=(x_1,\ldots,x_{N+1})$, $D=\prod_\lambda n_\lambda!$ refers to the full $N+1$-particle list, and $D_j=D/n_{\lambda_j}$ refers to the remaining list.

For each fixed $j$, relabel the remaining entries:

$$
(\mu_1,\ldots,\mu_N)
:=(\lambda_1,\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_j},\ldots,\lambda_{N+1}),
\qquad y_a:=x_{a+1}.
$$

To express the new label positions in terms of the old ones, write

$$
r_j(a):=\begin{cases}a,&a<j,\\a+1,&a\ge j,\end{cases}
\qquad \mu_a=\lambda_{r_j(a)},\qquad a=1,\ldots,N.
$$

For example, when $j=2$, the first two remaining labels are $\mu_1=\lambda_1$, $\mu_2=\lambda_3$, while the first two remaining coordinates are $y_1=x_2$, $y_2=x_3$. The label and coordinate indices do not have to match: they are relabeled separately.

Let $\sigma\in S_N$ run over all permutations of the $N$ remaining positions. Here $\eta^\sigma$ is $1$ for Bose and the parity sign of $\sigma$ for Fermi, as in [[1. Basic Space#^def-exchange-symmetry|exchange symmetry]]. By the induction hypothesis and the coordinate wavefunction formula,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&C_N(x_2,\ldots,x_{N+1};
\lambda_1,\ldots,\widehat{\lambda_j},\ldots,\lambda_{N+1})\\
&=\sqrt{N!}\,\dfrac1{\sqrt{N!D_j}}
\sum_{\sigma\in S_N}\eta^\sigma
\prod_{a=1}^N\varphi_{\mu_a}(y_{\sigma(a)})\\
&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{D_j}}
\sum_{\sigma\in S_N}\eta^\sigma
\prod_{a=1}^N\varphi_{\lambda_{r_j(a)}}(x_{\sigma(a)+1}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Since $n_{\lambda_j}D_j=D$, substitution into the recursion gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
&C_{N+1}(X;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{N+1})\\
&=\dfrac1{\sqrt D}
\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\sum_{\sigma\in S_N}
\eta^{j-1}\eta^\sigma\,
\varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
\prod_{a=1}^N\varphi_{\lambda_{r_j(a)}}(x_{\sigma(a)+1}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The two sums have different roles: $j$ chooses the label paired with $x_1$, and $\sigma$ assigns the remaining labels to $x_2,\ldots,x_{N+1}$. Together, they specify one full permutation $P\in S_{N+1}$:

$$
P(j)=1,\qquad P(r_j(a))=\sigma(a)+1.
$$

Conversely, each $P$ determines exactly one $j=P^{-1}(1)$ and one remaining permutation $\sigma$. Thus no full permutation is missed or counted twice, even when some Bose labels have equal values: the permutations act on positions.

The product becomes

$$
\varphi_{\lambda_j}(x_1)
\prod_{a=1}^N\varphi_{\lambda_{r_j(a)}}(x_{\sigma(a)+1})
=\prod_{i=1}^{N+1}\varphi_{\lambda_i}(x_{P(i)}).
$$

To check the sign, consider the list $(P(1),\ldots,P(N+1))$. The entry $1$ is at position $j$. Moving it to the front takes $j-1$ exchanges, and the remaining list is $(\sigma(1)+1,\ldots,\sigma(N)+1)$. Therefore $\eta^P=\eta^{j-1}\eta^\sigma$.

Combining the two sums into a sum over $P$,

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_{N+1}(X;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{N+1})
&=\dfrac1{\sqrt D}\sum_{P\in S_{N+1}}\eta^P
\prod_{i=1}^{N+1}\varphi_{\lambda_i}(x_{P(i)})\\
&=\sqrt{(N+1)!}\,
\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_{N+1}}(X).
\end{aligned}
$$

This completes the induction.

> [!done] Vacuum amplitude and normalized wavefunction
> For each normalized Bose or Fermi basis state with $N\ge1$,
> $$
> C_N(X;\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N)
> =\sqrt{N!}\,\Psi_{\lambda_1,\ldots,\lambda_N}(X).
> $$
^prop-vacuum-amplitude-wavefunction

### 5. Identifying the Map $U_N$

Now take an arbitrary $N$-particle state $|\Psi_N\rangle=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle$, using the independent basis labels defined above. The original definition of $U_N$ gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\bigl(U_N|\Psi_N\rangle\bigr)(X)
&=\sum_\alpha c_\alpha\Psi_\alpha(X)\\
&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{N!}}\sum_\alpha c_\alpha
\langle0|\hat\psi(x_N)\cdots\hat\psi(x_1)|\alpha\rangle\\
&=\dfrac1{\sqrt{N!}}
\langle0|\hat\psi(x_N)\cdots\hat\psi(x_1)
\left(\sum_\alpha c_\alpha|\alpha\rangle\right).
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!done] Field-operator expression for $U_N$
> For an $N$-particle state with $N\ge1$,
> $$
> \boxed{
> \bigl(U_N|\Psi_N\rangle\bigr)(X)
> =\dfrac1{\sqrt{N!}}
> \langle0|\hat\psi(x_N)\cdots\hat\psi(x_1)|\Psi_N\rangle.
> }
> $$
^prop-field-expression-unitary-map

This is the same map already defined on the basis: the field-operator expression gives the prescribed wavefunction for every basis vector, and hence for their linear combinations. The order is kept as written, with $\hat\psi(x_1)$ acting first; this fixes the sign in the Fermi case.
