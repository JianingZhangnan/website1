# Moving an Annihilation Operator through a Creation String

Use the [[6. Commutation Relations#^prop-mixed-relations|creation–annihilation relation]]

$$
a_\nu a_\mu^\dagger=\delta_{\nu\mu}I+\eta a_\mu^\dagger a_\nu,
\qquad \eta=+1\text{ (Bose)},\quad\eta=-1\text{ (Fermi)}.
$$

Here $I$ is the identity and $\delta_{\nu\mu}$ is the Kronecker delta. The vacuum obeys $a_\nu|0\rangle=0$ by [[5. Creation and Annihilation#1. If $N=0$|vacuum annihilation]].

> [!done] Annihilation through a creation string
> For $N\ge1$, let $b$ obey $b|0\rangle=0$ and
> $b a_\mu^\dagger=c_\mu I+\eta a_\mu^\dagger b$, where each $c_\mu$ is a scalar. Then
> $$
> b\,a_{\mu_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger|0\rangle
> =\sum_{i=1}^N\eta^{i-1}c_{\mu_i}
> a_{\mu_1}^\dagger\cdots\widehat{a_{\mu_i}^\dagger}\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger|0\rangle.
> $$
> A hat means omission; all remaining factors retain their order. The creation string is not normalized.
^prop-annihilation-through-creation-string

To prove it, expand the first pair:

$$
\begin{aligned}
b\,a_{\mu_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger|0\rangle
&=c_{\mu_1}a_{\mu_2}^\dagger\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger|0\rangle
+\eta a_{\mu_1}^\dagger b\,a_{\mu_2}^\dagger\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger|0\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$

Repeat on the remaining term. Reaching position $i$ contributes $\eta^{i-1}$; choosing the scalar term there deletes that creation operator. After all $N$ positions, the only leftover term is $\eta^N a_{\mu_1}^\dagger\cdots a_{\mu_N}^\dagger b|0\rangle=0$. This proves the formula.

## Mode and field substitutions

For $b=a_\nu$, take $c_\mu=\delta_{\nu\mu}$.

For the [[8. Field Operator#Definition|annihilation field]] $b=\hat\psi(x)=\sum_\nu\varphi_\nu(x)a_\nu$, take $c_\mu=\varphi_\mu(x)$, the single-particle wavefunction of mode $\mu$. Indeed,

$$
\hat\psi(x)a_\mu^\dagger
=\sum_\nu\varphi_\nu(x)(\delta_{\nu\mu}I+\eta a_\mu^\dagger a_\nu)
=\varphi_\mu(x)I+\eta a_\mu^\dagger\hat\psi(x),
$$

and $\hat\psi(x)|0\rangle=0$ follows term by term. Thus both applications use the same boxed identity. Apply it to finite mode expansions first; continuous-coordinate fields are understood through their wavefunction expansions.
