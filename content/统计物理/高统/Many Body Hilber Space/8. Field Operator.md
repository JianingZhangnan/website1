# Definition

$$
a_{x} := \hat{\psi}(x) := \sum_{\lambda} a_{\lambda} \langle x | \lambda \rangle
$$

Here $\{|\lambda\rangle\}$ is an orthonormal basis of the single-particle Hilbert space $\mathcal H$, and $|x\rangle$ is the single-particle position eigenstate. The coefficient $\langle x|\lambda\rangle = \phi_\lambda(x)$ is the single-particle wavefunction in coordinate representation.

Taking the Hermitian conjugate gives the creation field operator:
$$
a_x^\dagger := \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) = \sum_\lambda a_\lambda^\dagger \langle \lambda | x \rangle.
$$

>[!done] Number Operator 
>$$
>\begin{align}
>\int \mathrm{d}x \hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(x)\hat{\psi}(x)  & = \sum_{\lambda}\sum_{\mu >}a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}a_{\mu}\int \braket{ \lambda | x }\braket{ x | \mu >}\,\mathrm{d}x \\
 >& =\sum_{\lambda,\mu}a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}a_{\mu}\braket{ \lambda | \mu  } \\
 >& =\sum_{\lambda}a^{\dagger}_{\lambda}a_{\lambda}=\hat{N}
>\end{align}
>$$
^prop-field-number-operator


# Decomposition Under the Direct Sum

## 1. Resolution of Identity and Block Decomposition

From [[4. Fock Space#^prop-fock-completeness|the completeness relation in Fock space]], the identity operator decomposes into orthogonal projectors onto the $N$-particle sectors:
$$
I = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} P_N,
$$
where each $P_N$ can be expressed in terms of creation and annihilation operators via [[4. Fock Space#Operator representation via creation and annihilation operators|the vacuum projector representation]]:
$$
P_N = \frac{1}{N!} \sum_{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_N} a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger |0\rangle\langle0| a_{\lambda_N} \cdots a_{\lambda_1} \quad (N \ge 1), \qquad P_0 = |0\rangle\langle0|.
$$

Inserting the identity operator on both sides of $\hat{\psi}(x)$:
$$
\hat{\psi}(x) = I \hat{\psi}(x) I = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} \sum_{M=0}^{\infty} P_N \hat{\psi}(x) P_M.
$$

## 2. Selection Rule: $M = N + 1$

Substituting the explicit operator expressions of $P_N$, $P_M$, and $\hat{\psi}(x) = \sum_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle a_\lambda$:
$$
P_N \hat{\psi}(x) P_M = \frac{1}{N! M!} \sum_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle \sum_{\substack{\lambda_1,\dots,\lambda_N \\ \mu_1,\dots,\mu_M}} \left( a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger |0\rangle \right) \underbrace{\langle 0| a_{\lambda_N} \cdots a_{\lambda_1} a_\lambda a_{\mu_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\mu_M}^\dagger |0\rangle}_{\mathcal{V}_{N,M}} \left( \langle 0| a_{\mu_M} \cdots a_{\mu_1} \right).
$$

Notice the central **vacuum expectation value**:
$$
\mathcal{V}_{N,M} = \langle 0| \underbrace{a_{\lambda_N} \cdots a_{\lambda_1} a_\lambda}_{N+1 \text{ annihilations}} \ \underbrace{a_{\mu_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\mu_M}^\dagger}_{M \text{ creations}} |0\rangle.
$$
- The bra $\langle 0| a_{\lambda_N} \cdots a_{\lambda_1} a_\lambda = \left( a_\lambda^\dagger a_{\lambda_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\lambda_N}^\dagger |0\rangle \right)^\dagger$ is the dual of an $(N+1)$-particle state.
- The ket $a_{\mu_1}^\dagger \cdots a_{\mu_M}^\dagger |0\rangle$ is an $M$-particle state.
- Since subspaces with different particle numbers are orthogonal, the inner product vanishes unless the number of creation operators equals the number of annihilation operators:
  $$
  \mathcal{V}_{N,M} = 0 \quad \text{whenever } M \neq N + 1.
  $$

Therefore, all blocks with $M \neq N+1$ vanish identically:
$$
P_N \hat{\psi}(x) P_M = 0 \quad (M \neq N + 1).
$$

Taking the Hermitian conjugate gives $P_M \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_N = 0$ for $M \ne N+1$, so the double sums collapse to sums over adjacent sectors:

> [!done] Sector decomposition of field operators
> $$
> \hat{\psi}(x) = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} P_N \hat{\psi}(x) P_{N+1}, \qquad
> \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) = \sum_{N=0}^{\infty} P_{N+1} \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_N.
> $$
^prop-field-sector-decomposition

## 3. Block Matrix Form

With respect to the direct sum decomposition of Fock space $\mathcal X = \mathcal X^0 \oplus \mathcal X^1 \oplus \mathcal X^2 \oplus \cdots$, the annihilation field operator $\hat{\psi}(x)$ is strictly superdiagonal, lowering the particle number by 1 ($\mathcal X^{N+1} \to \mathcal X^N$), while the creation field operator $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)$ is strictly subdiagonal, raising the particle number by 1 ($\mathcal X^N \to \mathcal X^{N+1}$):

> [!done] Block matrix representation of field operators
> $$
> \hat{\psi}(x) =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & \boxed{P_0 \hat{\psi}(x) P_1} & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
> 0 & 0 & P_1 \hat{\psi}(x) P_2 & 0 & \cdots \\
> 0 & 0 & 0 & P_2 \hat{\psi}(x) P_3 & \cdots \\
> \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots
> \end{pmatrix}
> \begin{matrix}
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^0 \\
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^1 \\
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^2 \\
> \vdots
> \end{matrix}
> $$
> $$
> \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) =
> \begin{pmatrix}
> 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
> \boxed{P_1 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_0} & 0 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
> 0 & P_2 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_1 & 0 & 0 & \cdots \\
> 0 & 0 & P_3 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_2 & 0 & \cdots \\
> \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & \ddots
> \end{pmatrix}
> \begin{matrix}
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^0 \\
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^1 \\
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^2 \\
> \leftarrow \mathcal X^3 \\
> \vdots
> \end{matrix}
> $$
^prop-field-block-matrix

## 4. Evaluation of the First Block ($1 \leftrightarrow 0$)

The first non-zero block connects the 1-particle sector $\mathcal X^1$ and the vacuum $\mathcal X^0$:
$$
P_0 \hat{\psi}(x) P_1 = |0\rangle\langle0| \left( \sum_\lambda a_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle \right) \left( \sum_{\lambda'} |\lambda'\rangle\langle\lambda'| \right).
$$
Since $a_\lambda |\lambda'\rangle = \delta_{\lambda,\lambda'} |0\rangle$, we have:
$$
\begin{aligned}
P_0 \hat{\psi}(x) P_1
&= |0\rangle \sum_{\lambda,\lambda'} \langle x|\lambda\rangle \, \delta_{\lambda,\lambda'} \langle0|0\rangle \, \langle\lambda'| \\
&= |0\rangle \sum_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle \langle\lambda|.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the single-particle completeness relation $\sum_\lambda |\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda| = I_{\mathcal H}$ (or $\langle x| = \sum_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda|$), this reduces to $|0\rangle\langle x|$. Taking the Hermitian conjugate gives the corresponding block for $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)$:

> [!done] First block of the field operators
> $$
> P_0 \hat{\psi}(x) P_1 = |0\rangle \langle x|, \qquad P_1 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_0 = |x\rangle \langle 0|.
> $$
^prop-field-operator-first-block

Acting on an arbitrary single-particle state $|\phi\rangle \in \mathcal X^1$:
$$
\hat{\psi}(x) |\phi\rangle = P_0 \hat{\psi}(x) P_1 |\phi\rangle = |0\rangle \langle x|\phi\rangle = \phi(x) |0\rangle.
$$
The first block annihilates the particle at position $x$, returning the vacuum $|0\rangle$ with an amplitude equal to the single-particle wave function $\phi(x)$.

# Properties of Field Operator

## 1. Operation on Vacuum

With the block matrix form established, the action on the vacuum $|0\rangle = \begin{pmatrix} |0\rangle & 0 & 0 & \cdots \end{pmatrix}^T \in \mathcal X^0$ becomes immediate:

### 1. Annihilation on Vacuum
Since the first column of the superdiagonal matrix $\hat{\psi}(x)$ is identically zero (or $P_N|0\rangle = 0$ for all $N \ge 1$):

> [!done] Vacuum is annihilated by $\hat{\psi}(x)$
> $$
> \hat{\psi}(x)|0\rangle = 0.
> $$
^prop-vacuum-annihilation

Conversely, if a state $|\nu\rangle$ satisfies $\hat{\psi}(x)|\nu\rangle = 0$ for all $x$, then $\sum_\lambda a_\lambda|\nu\rangle \langle x|\lambda\rangle = 0$. By the linear independence of the wavefunctions $\{\langle x|\lambda\rangle\}$, this implies $a_\lambda|\nu\rangle = 0$ for all $\lambda$, so $|\nu\rangle \propto |0\rangle$.

### 2. Creation on Vacuum
Using the first subdiagonal block $P_1 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_0 = |x\rangle\langle0|$:

> [!done] Creation of a localized single-particle state
> $$
> \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)|0\rangle = P_1 \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) P_0 |0\rangle = |x\rangle\langle0|0\rangle = |x\rangle.
> $$
^prop-vacuum-creation

Thus, $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)$ acting on the vacuum creates a single-particle state precisely localized at position $x$.

## 2. Commutation Relations

Let $[A, B]_\mp := AB \mp BA$, where the upper sign ($-$) denotes the commutator $[A, B]$ for Bose statistics, and the lower sign ($+$) denotes the anticommutator $\{A, B\}$ for Fermi statistics.

> [!done] Commutation relations of field operators
> For both Bose ($-$) and Fermi ($+$) statistics:
> $$
> [\hat{\psi}(x),\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(x')]_{\mp}=\delta(x-x'),\qquad [\hat{\psi}(x),\hat{\psi}(x')]_{\mp}=0,\qquad [\hat{\psi}^{\dagger}(x),\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')]_{\mp}=0.
> $$
^prop-field-commutation-relations

**Proof**.
Expanding $\hat{\psi}(x)$ and $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')$ via their definitions $\hat{\psi}(x) = \sum_\lambda a_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle$ and $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x') = \sum_\mu a_\mu^\dagger \langle \mu|x'\rangle$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')]_\mp
&= \left[ \sum_\lambda a_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle, \sum_\mu a_\mu^\dagger \langle \mu|x'\rangle \right]_\mp \\
&= \sum_{\lambda,\mu} [a_\lambda, a_\mu^\dagger]_\mp \langle x|\lambda\rangle \langle \mu|x'\rangle.
\end{aligned}
$$
Using the canonical commutation relations $[a_\lambda, a_\mu^\dagger]_\mp = \delta_{\lambda,\mu}$ (see [[6. Commutation Relations#^prop-mixed-relations|creation and annihilation relations]]) and the single-particle completeness relation $\sum_\lambda |\lambda\rangle\langle\lambda| = I_{\mathcal H}$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')]_\mp
&= \sum_{\lambda,\mu} \delta_{\lambda,\mu} \langle x|\lambda\rangle \langle \mu|x'\rangle \\
&= \sum_\lambda \langle x|\lambda\rangle \langle \lambda|x'\rangle \\
&= \left\langle x \left| \left( \sum_\lambda |\lambda\rangle\langle \lambda| \right) \right| x' \right\rangle \\
&= \langle x|x'\rangle = \delta(x-x').
\end{aligned}
$$
Similarly, using $[a_\lambda, a_\mu]_\mp = 0$ and $[a_\lambda^\dagger, a_\mu^\dagger]_\mp = 0$ from [[6. Commutation Relations#^prop-creation-annihilation-separate|creations and annihilations separate]]:
$$
[\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}(x')]_\mp = \sum_{\lambda,\mu} [a_\lambda, a_\mu]_\mp \langle x|\lambda\rangle \langle x'|\mu\rangle = 0,
$$
$$
[\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')]_\mp = \sum_{\lambda,\mu} [a_\lambda^\dagger, a_\mu^\dagger]_\mp \langle \lambda|x\rangle \langle \mu|x'\rangle = 0.
$$
$\blacksquare$

Explicitly:
- **Bose fields** satisfy canonical commutation relations:
  $$
  [\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')] = \delta(x-x'), \qquad [\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}(x')] = 0, \qquad [\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')] = 0.
  $$
- **Fermi fields** satisfy canonical anticommutation relations:
  $$
  \{\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')\} = \delta(x-x'), \qquad \{\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}(x')\} = 0, \qquad \{\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')\} = 0.
  $$

## 3. Commutation with Total Number Operator and Generalization

Using the total number operator $\hat{N} = \int \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')\hat{\psi}(x')\,\mathrm{d}x'$ (see [[#^prop-field-number-operator|number operator in field representation]] or [[7. Number Operator#^def-total-number-operator|total number operator]]) and the commutator identity $[A, BC] = [A, B]_\mp C \pm B [A, C]_\mp$ (which expands directly via $(AB \mp BA)C \pm B(AC \mp CA) = ABC - BCA = [A, BC]$; see [[量子力学/Hilbert空间/对易子与反对易子#对易子|commutator product rules]]):
$$
\begin{aligned}
[\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{N}]
&= \int \mathrm{d}x' [\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')\hat{\psi}(x')] \\
&= \int \mathrm{d}x' \Big( [\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x')]_\mp \hat{\psi}(x') \pm \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x') \underbrace{[\hat{\psi}(x), \hat{\psi}(x')]_\mp}_{0} \Big) \\
&= \int \mathrm{d}x' \delta(x-x') \hat{\psi}(x') = \hat{\psi}(x).
\end{aligned}
$$
Therefore:
$$
[\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}(x)] = -\hat{\psi}(x) \iff \hat{N}\hat{\psi}(x) = \hat{\psi}(x)(\hat{N}-1).
$$
Taking the Hermitian conjugate (or directly evaluating $[\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x), \hat{N}]$):
$$
[\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)] = \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) \iff \hat{N}\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) = \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)(\hat{N}+1).
$$

> [!done] Commutation with total number operator
> $$
> [\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}(x)] = -\hat{\psi}(x), \qquad [\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)] = \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x),
> $$
> or equivalently:
> $$
> \hat{N}\hat{\psi}(x) = \hat{\psi}(x)(\hat{N}-1), \qquad \hat{N}\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x) = \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x)(\hat{N}+1).
> $$
^prop-field-number-commutation

### Inductive Generalization for Multi-Field Products

Applying the relation repeatedly to a string of $m$ field operators ($m \in \mathbb{Z}^+$):

> [!done] Inductive shift for multi-field products
> For any positive integer $m$:
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \hat{N} \hat{\psi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(x_m) &= \hat{\psi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(x_m)(\hat{N}-m), \\
> \hat{N} \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m) &= \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)(\hat{N}+m).
> \end{aligned}
> $$
> Equivalently:
> $$
> [\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(x_m)] = -m\,\hat{\psi}(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}(x_m), \qquad [\hat{N}, \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)] = +m\,\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m).
> $$
^prop-field-inductive-number

**Proof by induction**.
The base case $m=1$ is given above. Assuming the relation holds for $m$:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\hat{N} \left(\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1})\right)
&= \left(\hat{N} \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)\right) \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1}) \\
&= \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)(\hat{N}+m) \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1}) \\
&= \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m) \left( \hat{N}\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1}) + m\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1}) \right) \\
&= \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m) \left( \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1})(\hat{N}+1) + m\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1}) \right) \\
&= \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_{m+1})(\hat{N}+m+1).
\end{aligned}
$$
The annihilation formula follows analogously (or by taking Hermitian conjugates). $\blacksquare$

In particular, acting on the vacuum $|0\rangle$ where $\hat{N}|0\rangle = 0$:
$$
\hat{N} \left( \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)|0\rangle \right) = m \left( \hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)|0\rangle \right).
$$
Hence $\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_1)\cdots\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_m)|0\rangle$ is an eigenstate of the total number operator $\hat{N}$ with eigenvalue $m$, corresponding to an $m$-particle localized state. For Fermi statistics, because $(\hat{\psi}^\dagger(x_i))^2 = 0$ by the [[3. Basis of Bose and Fermi Spaces#^prop-pauli-exclusion|Pauli exclusion principle]], this state is non-trivial if and only if all coordinates are pairwise distinct ($x_i \ne x_j$ for $i \ne j$).
