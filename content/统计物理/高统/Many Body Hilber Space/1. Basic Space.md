# Basic Space

> [!def] $N$-particle Hilbert space and product state
> $$
> \mathcal H^N:=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\mathcal H_i,\qquad
> |\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N):=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}|\lambda_i\rangle_i.
> $$
> Here $i$ labels a particle slot. For identical particles, all $\mathcal H_i$ are copies of the same single-particle Hilbert space $\mathcal H$.
^def-basic-space

If $\{|\lambda_i\rangle_i\}$ is an orthonormal basis of $\mathcal H_i$, then $\{|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)\}$ is an orthonormal [[张量积的定义#^def-tensor-product-basis|product basis]] of $\mathcal H^N$. A general state is a linear combination of these basis states (including norm limits when the space is infinite-dimensional).

Here round kets $|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)$ denote product states; angle kets $|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N\rangle$ will denote the normalized Bose or Fermi states constructed later.

### Example: Two qubits (here ignoring identicality)

$$
\mathcal H^2=\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2.
$$

Take the basis to be $|\lambda_i\rangle_i=|0\rangle_i$, $|\mu_i\rangle_i=|1\rangle_i$, for $i=1,2$. Then the basis of the product space is

$$
\begin{aligned}
|\lambda_1\lambda_2)&=|00),&|\lambda_1\mu_2)&=|01),\\
|\mu_1\lambda_2)&=|10),&|\mu_1\mu_2)&=|11).
\end{aligned}
$$

## 乘积基的正交归一性与完备性

### Orthogonality of the basis (more precisely, orthonormality)

> [!done] Orthonormality of the product basis
> $$
> (\lambda'_1\cdots\lambda'_N|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)
> =\prod_{i=1}^{N}{}_i\langle\lambda'_i|\lambda_i\rangle_i
> =\prod_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_i,\lambda'_i}.
> $$
^prop-product-orthonormality

The inner product of product states is the product of the single-particle inner products; see [[运算性质#^prop-inner-product|积态的内积性质]].

### Completeness of the basis

> [!done] Completeness of the product basis
> $$
> \sum_{\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N}
> |\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N|=I_{\mathcal H^N}.
> $$
> The sum means $N$ independent sums: $\sum_{\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N}=\sum_{\lambda_1}\cdots\sum_{\lambda_N}$.
^prop-product-completeness

The [[量子力学/Hilbert空间/简介#^prop-orthonormal-expansion|single-particle basis expansion]] gives $\sum_{\lambda_i}|\lambda_i\rangle_i{}_i\langle\lambda_i|=I_{\mathcal H_i}$: acting on any vector returns that vector. Taking their tensor product,

$$
I_{\mathcal H^N}
=\bigotimes_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{\lambda_i}|\lambda_i\rangle_i{}_i\langle\lambda_i|\right)
=\sum_{\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N}|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)(\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N|.
$$

For the two-qubit example,

$$
|00)(00|+|01)(01|+|10)(10|+|11)(11|=I.
$$

# Permutation Operator

> [!def] Permutation operator
> $P_{ij}$ exchanges particle slots $i$ and $j$ in a product state. A general permutation $P\in S_N$ reorders the slots:
> $$
> P|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)=|\lambda_{P1}\cdots\lambda_{PN}).
> $$
> Here $\lambda_{Pi}$ denotes the label in slot $i$ after applying $P$; $S_N$ is the group of all $N!$ permutations of the slots. Operator products act from right to left.
^def-permutation-operator

For identical Bose or Fermi particles, [[基础公设#^def-identical-particle-permutation|exchanging two particles does not change the physical state]]. By the [[基础公设#^def-pure-state-postulate|state postulate]], normalized vectors differing by an overall phase represent the same physical state. So, initially with $\xi_{ij}$ a phase factor,

$$
P_{ij}|\psi)=\xi_{ij}|\psi).
$$

Since $P_{ij}^2=I$,

$$
|\psi)=P_{ij}^2|\psi)=\xi_{ij}^2|\psi)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad \xi_{ij}=\pm1.
$$

For one certain kind of particle, the sign is fixed for every $i,j$. For example,

$$
P_{12}=P_{13}P_{32}P_{13}
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\xi_{12}=\xi_{13}^2\xi_{32}=\xi_{32}.
$$

Any two exchanges are related in this way (by relabeling the slots). The same sign must therefore be used throughout the chosen Bose or Fermi space.

> [!def] Bose and Fermi exchange symmetry
> $$
> P|\psi)=(\pm)^P|\psi),\qquad P\in S_N.
> $$
> The upper sign is for Bose, the lower sign for Fermi. $(+)^P=1$; $(-)^P=(-1)^P$ is $+1$ for an even permutation and $-1$ for an odd permutation. Even/odd means an even/odd number of two-slot exchanges in a decomposition of $P$.
^def-exchange-symmetry

This is the **symmetrization postulate** for the Bose and Fermi cases considered here. The $\pm1$ above is the eigenvalue on the corresponding physical space, rather than the permutation operator on all of $\mathcal H^N$.

The parity does not depend on the chosen exchange decomposition: in the corresponding permutation matrix, each exchange interchanges two rows and changes the determinant by $-1$. Thus a decomposition with $k$ exchanges has sign $(-1)^k$, fixed by that determinant. The sign is multiplicative, as in the sign and determinant examples in [[Group/1. Foundations/2. Homomorphism#Basic Definations|homomorphism]].

## Properties of permutation

### 1. Unitarity

Unitarity means $P^\dagger P=PP^\dagger=I$.

Applying the same permutation to both product states merely reorders the factors in their inner product:

$$
\begin{aligned}
(\lambda'_1\cdots\lambda'_N|P^\dagger P|\lambda_1\cdots\lambda_N)
&=(\lambda'_{P1}\cdots\lambda'_{PN}|\lambda_{P1}\cdots\lambda_{PN})\\
&=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_{Pi},\lambda'_{Pi}}
=\prod_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{\lambda_i,\lambda'_i}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Since any state can be expanded in the product basis, $(u|P^\dagger P|v)=(u|v)$ for arbitrary $u,v$. Also, $P^{-1}$ undoes the permutation. Therefore:

> [!done] Unitarity of permutation
> $$
> P^\dagger P=PP^\dagger=I,\qquad P^\dagger=P^{-1}.
> $$
^prop-permutation-unitarity

### 2. All physical observables commute with permutation

For identical particles, relabeling all particle slots leaves a physical observable $O$ unchanged. In operator form, this requirement is $POP^\dagger=O$; equivalently, $(u|POP^\dagger|v)=(u|O|v)$ for arbitrary $u,v\in\mathcal H^N$.

Multiplying $POP^\dagger=O$ on the right by $P$, and using $P^\dagger P=I$, gives $PO=OP$.

> [!done] Permutation invariance of physical observables
> For a physical observable of identical particles,
> $$
> [O,P]=0.
> $$
> Especially, $[H,P]=0$, where $H$ is the Hamiltonian. Here $[A,B]=AB-BA$ is the commutator; see [[对易子与反对易子#对易子|对易子]].
^prop-observable-permutation
