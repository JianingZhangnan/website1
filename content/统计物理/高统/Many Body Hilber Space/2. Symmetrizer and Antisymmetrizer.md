# Symmetrizer and Antisymmetrizer

According to the [[1. Basic Space#^def-exchange-symmetry|symmetrization postulate]], not every state in the basic space is physical. Only the states satisfying $P|\psi)=(\pm)^P|\psi)$ are physically realizable in the Bose or Fermi case.

The tensor product Hilbert space is too large for these physical states. Its product basis is orthonormal; the redundancy appears when every ordered product state is projected onto the physical space. So we have to construct a suitable space.

> [!def] Bose space and Fermi space
> $$
> \begin{aligned}
> \mathcal B^N&:=\{|\psi)\in\mathcal H^N:P|\psi)=|\psi),\ \forall P\in S_N\},\\
> \mathcal F^N&:=\{|\psi)\in\mathcal H^N:P|\psi)=(-1)^P|\psi),\ \forall P\in S_N\}.
> \end{aligned}
> $$
^def-bose-fermi-space

> [!def] Symmetrizer and antisymmetrizer
> $$
> S_{b/f}:=\dfrac{1}{N!}\sum_{P\in S_N}(\pm)^P P.
> $$
> $S_b$ is the symmetrizer; $S_f$ is the antisymmetrizer. They may also be denoted by $S$ and $A$, respectively.
^def-symmetrizer

### Example: Two-body

$$
S_b=\dfrac12(I+P_{12}),\qquad S_f=\dfrac12(I-P_{12}).
$$

### Example: Three-body

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_b&=\dfrac16(I+P_{12}+P_{13}+P_{23}+P_{123}+P_{132}),\\
S_f&=\dfrac16(I+P_{123}+P_{132}-P_{12}-P_{13}-P_{23}).
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $P_{123}$ and $P_{132}$ are the two three-cycles: $1\to2\to3\to1$ and its inverse. Each is the product of two exchanges, hence even.

Here we want to prove $S_b\mathcal H^N=\mathcal B^N$ and $S_f\mathcal H^N=\mathcal F^N$.

## Properties of $S_{b/f}$

### 1. Hermiticity

Hermiticity means $S_{b/f}^\dagger=S_{b/f}$. Use the [[量子力学/Hilbert空间/简介#^prop-adjoint-algebra|algebra of adjoints]] and [[1. Basic Space#^prop-permutation-unitarity|unitarity of permutation]], $P^\dagger=P^{-1}$. As $P$ runs over $S_N$, so does $P^{-1}$. Their signs are the same because $(\pm)^P(\pm)^{P^{-1}}=(\pm)^I=1$. Thus

$$
S_{b/f}^\dagger
=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P(\pm)^P P^{-1}
=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_R(\pm)^R R
=S_{b/f}.
$$

> [!done] Hermiticity of symmetrizer and antisymmetrizer
> $$
> S_{b/f}^\dagger=S_{b/f}.
> $$
^prop-symmetrizer-hermiticity

### 2. Projection

Let $Q\in S_N$. The [[1. Basic Definitions and Examples#^f7d628|rearrangement lemma]] says that $QP$ runs over the whole group when $P$ does. The parity of a product is the sum of the two parities modulo $2$, so $(\pm)^{QP}=(\pm)^Q(\pm)^P$. Hence

$$
\begin{aligned}
QS_{b/f}
&=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P(\pm)^P QP\\
&=(\pm)^Q\dfrac1{N!}\sum_{R=QP}(\pm)^R R
=(\pm)^Q S_{b/f}.
\end{aligned}
$$

The same reordering on the right gives $S_{b/f}Q=(\pm)^Q S_{b/f}$. Therefore

$$
\begin{aligned}
S_{b/f}^2
&=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P(\pm)^P PS_{b/f}\\
&=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P(\pm)^{2P}S_{b/f}
=S_{b/f}.
\end{aligned}
$$

> [!done] Projection and permutation properties
> $$
> S_{b/f}^2=S_{b/f},\qquad
> QS_{b/f}=S_{b/f}Q=(\pm)^Q S_{b/f}.
> $$
> $S_{b/f}$ are projections: they leave their image space unchanged, since $S_{b/f}(S_{b/f}|v))=S_{b/f}|v)$.
^prop-symmetrizer-projection

### 3. Bose space and Fermi space

If $|\psi)\in\mathcal B^N$, then

$$
S_b|\psi)=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P P|\psi)=|\psi),
$$

so $\mathcal B^N\subseteq S_b\mathcal H^N$.

Conversely, if $|\psi)\in S_b\mathcal H^N$, there exists $|\zeta)\in\mathcal H^N$ such that $|\psi)=S_b|\zeta)$. Then

$$
P|\psi)=PS_b|\zeta)=S_b|\zeta)=|\psi),
$$

so $S_b\mathcal H^N\subseteq\mathcal B^N$.

For Fermi, if $P|\psi)=(-1)^P|\psi)$ for every $P$, then

$$
S_f|\psi)=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P(-1)^P P|\psi)
=\dfrac1{N!}\sum_P|\psi)=|\psi).
$$

Conversely, $|\psi)=S_f|\zeta)$ implies

$$
P|\psi)=PS_f|\zeta)=(-1)^P S_f|\zeta)=(-1)^P|\psi).
$$

> [!done] Bose space and Fermi space are the projection images
> $$
> \mathcal B^N=S_b\mathcal H^N=\operatorname{range}S_b,\qquad
> \mathcal F^N=S_f\mathcal H^N=\operatorname{range}S_f.
> $$
> Here $\operatorname{range}T=\{T|v):|v)\in\mathcal H^N\}$ is the image of $T$ (also written $\operatorname{Im}T$).
^prop-physical-space-image
