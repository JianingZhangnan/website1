>[!def] permutation
>A *permutation* of a set $S$ is a bijective(invertible) map from $S$ to itself.

If we take any vector space $V$ just as an infinite set, any **invertible** [[3A Vector Space of Linear Maps#^linearmap|linear map]] is a permutation that preserves addition and scalar multiplication.

There is a *permutation matrix* $P$ associated to any permutation $p$. For example,  if there are three indices, the matrix $P$ associated to the cyclic permutation $p=(\mathbf{1,2,3})$ and its operation on a column vector are as follows:
$$
PX = \begin{pmatrix}
0 & 0 & 1 \\
1 & 0 & 0 \\
0 & 1 & 0
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}
x_{1} \\
x_{2} \\
x_{3}
\end{pmatrix}
= \begin{pmatrix}
x_{3} \\
x_{1} \\
x_{2}
\end{pmatrix}
$$

It is essential to write the matrix of an arbitrary permutation down carefully, and to check that the matrix associated to a product $pq$ of permutations is the product matrix $PQ$. To write a permutation matrix explicitly, it is best to use the $n\times n$ [[Basic Tricks of Matrix#^70ece7|matrix units]] $e_{ij}$:
$$
P = \sum_{i} e_{pi,i} 
$$
It maps position $i$ to $p(i)$:
$$
PX = \left( \sum_{i}e_{pi,i} \right)\left( \sum_{j}e_{j}x_{j} \right) = \sum_{i,j}e_{pi,i}e_{j}x_{j} = \sum_{i}e_{pi,i}e_{i}x_{i}=\sum_{i}e_{pi}x_{i}
$$
